# Second pilot, November 2026 (corpus v2) — plan frozen before the first review

**Status: exploratory, frozen before any model saw corpus v2.** Same protocol, models,
transport and settings as [the first pilot](PILOT_2026-11.md); only the corpus changes.

## Why a second corpus

The first pilot reached the ceiling: in all four arms, 15/15 planted defects were blocked
and identified and 0/15 controls were blocked. The repository's own rule applies: keep that
observation, do not obscure contracts, and move to cases selected before any score.

Corpus v2 (`fixtures-v2/`, 10 pairs, h01–h10) was written by the repository's engineering
agent immediately after the first pilot, from general knowledge of review failure modes:
omissions (a missing cache invalidation), effects across functions (a ledger that can now
reject an entry after balances moved), iterator consumption, signed rounding symmetry,
sort stability, Unicode case folding, retry and calendar edge cases, path containment and
first-versus-last deduplication. Every rule the witness tests check is written in the
contract. No case was tried on any model before this plan was frozen, and none will be
edited after the first request. All ten pairs pass `rbs verify-fixtures --corpus fixtures-v2`.

## Design

Configuration: `configs/pilot-local-v2.json` (seed 20261111). Transport:
`configs/transport.ollama-pilot.json`, unchanged. Arms: solo_blind (A), panel_blind (A×3),
panel_informed (A×3 with author note), panel_mixed (A, B, C). 10 pairs × 2 variants ×
10 reviewer jobs = 200 jobs, up to three concurrent requests, no retries.

## Measures, adjudication and limits

Identical to the first pilot plan. In addition, because v2 cases are harder, blocks for
another reason and control rejections are expected; each control rejection is triaged in
the adjudication rationale as a false alarm, a plausible real issue or a contract ambiguity.

Added limit: the corpus author is also the judge and knows which failure modes were
intended. The cases were not validated by an independent reviewer before use. A null or
ceiling result is reported as such; a high miss rate on one case may reflect a contract
that is harder to read rather than a reviewer blind spot, and is discussed case by case.
