# Making a project worth reusing

## Signal sought

A precise question, a small implementation, a contestable protocol, reproducible results
and a conclusion limited to what was observed. The aim is to show research engineering
and experimental design, not to create an artificial impression of a breakthrough.

## Conditions before an empirical publication

The README separates simulation, software tests, pilot and confirmatory study. Models and
parameters are exact. Cases, versions, selections and exclusions are documented. Judging
is audited. Raw artifacts and derived metrics are linked by hashes. Known and unknown
costs are visible. Intervals state their unit. Threats from contamination, artificial
difficulty, imperfect controls and selection of complete trials sit next to the results,
not in an appendix.

The license is MIT under a generic contributor attribution. Do not copy professional code
or an external corpus without appropriate rights. Model names imply no affiliation with
the labs concerned.

## Suggested report structure

Question and practical decision; closest work; intervention and information flow; corpus
and controls; frozen plan; results with denominators; three audited counterexamples;
costs; sensitivity analysis; failures and limits; reproduction and next question.
"Inconclusive on this sample" is better than a manufactured winner. A null result is
interesting only if its protocol and domain of usefulness are clear; absence of a
difference does not prove equivalence.

## Outreach

First ask an evaluation or review-tool maintainer to try the harness on a concrete
technical question. Then propose a small, useful integration or upstream pull request.
A public demonstration must be runnable and clearly separate from model scores.

The most credible message is "here is a tested hypothesis, what it explains, what refutes
it and how to reproduce it". Interest from any lab is unpredictable and is never promised.
